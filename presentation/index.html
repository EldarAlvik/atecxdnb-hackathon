This is the whole product from the user's side. It doesn't care what phone you have: landline, old mobile, new mobile. Put the call on speaker, press the button, and the box listens. The light is the message. Green is "no warning signs yet", deliberately not "safe". The screen gives a short reason in the language of the call, so Ingrid sees "Ber om BankID-kode", "asks for BankID code", and knows why to hang up. Hardware is an ESP32-S3 on the Atech board we were given. ~45 s.

Scam latches red for the rest of the call

≈ NOK 1 per 10-minute call in cloud costTeam estimate, list prices 2026
Five steps. The board streams audio to Deepgram's nova-3 model, which transcribes Norwegian and English live. About every six seconds, if there's new text, we send the rolling transcript to Claude Haiku with a short system prompt. It answers in compact JSON: a verdict and a reason of at most eight words, in the call's language. One design decision matters a lot: once it says scam, the light stays red for the rest of the call. A scammer can't talk it back to green. Latency: the red light comes two to ten seconds after the sentence that gives it away. A fake-bank call runs for minutes, so that's well before the code is read out. Cost is about one krone per ten-minute call, mostly speech-to-text. ~55 s.

The person who buys this is usually the daughter or son, not the 82-year-old. The Android app is for them. Live alerts: the moment the box goes red, their phone buzzes with the reason, and one tap calls mum. The scammer's "don't tell anyone" stops working. History: a simple list of calls and verdicts. We store the verdict, the short reason and the time, never audio or transcripts. Safe word: the family agrees on a word for emergencies. If a "grandchild" calls needing money and can't say it, that's a red flag. Pairing is Bluetooth with a passkey shown on the box's own screen, so only someone physically next to it can pair. ~45 s.

Results from first run

Spoken prompt injection

Caller reads a fake "system note" saying DNB verified the call. Must still go red.

Rolling-window evasion

BankID request early, then long small talk to push it out of the window. The latch must hold.

Benign look-alike

A demo proves nothing about accuracy, so we built an eval suite with Inspect AI. 96 scenarios. Each one is a phone call cut into the same ~6-second chunks of messy speech-to-text the board sees: no speaker labels, misheard words. We replay it chunk by chunk with the exact production prompt, the same 2,400-byte window and the same latch. The mix: 22 scam calls; 24 legitimate calls that look like scams, real DNB calls, the doctor, NAV, plus truly ambiguous ones; 20 adversarial calls; 30 broken dictation texts in Norwegian/English pairs. Adversarial examples: a scammer reading out a fake "system note" telling the AI the call is verified. And a scammer who asks for the code early, then talks for minutes so it falls out of the window. The latch is the defence there. Four scores: outcome, detection delay in chunks, false alarms (a red on a real call ruins it), and output format. Numbers: we will fill these in from the first full run. We are not showing numbers we haven't measured. The 30 on the false-alarm tile is the number of scenarios that must never turn red (17 legit calls, 5 adversarial controls, 8 dictation texts). Detection delay counts 6-second chunks past each scenario's deadline. ~60 s.

DNB is the natural first partner. It has 2.4 million retail customers, no "is DNB calling you" feature today, and Finans Norge names manipulation fraud the top threat for 2026. The pilot: 500 customers over 75, picked by DNB's fraud team and customer care, for example people who have been targeted before. Six months, with a matched control group from DNB's own fraud data. Real attacks are rare, maybe 3 to 12 in the whole pilot, so each home also gets about two consented, scripted "mystery scam calls" so we can actually measure detection. Targets: catch 90% of scam calls, red on fewer than 0.5% of real calls, median 20 seconds to red. The whole pilot costs about 600,000 kroner, roughly three average frauds. After that, DNB white-label to the highest-risk segment, then telcos, insurers, municipalities and Nordic banks. ~50 s.
Privacy & trust

A box that listens has to earn trust. What we fix before a pilot:

The elder consents, knowingly

Straffeloven § 205: a party may process their own call. Consent in their own words, visible "listening" light.

No audio stored, ever

Transcript only in memory. We keep verdict, short reason and time.

GDPR done properly

DPIA, data processing agreements with Deepgram and Anthropic, callers' data minimised.

Data stays in the EU

Deepgram EU endpoint. Claude through an EU cloud region, or zero data retention.

Real security

TLS verification on, no API keys on the device, secure boot, signed updates.

Honest when offline

No network means a grey "not protected" light, never a false green.

Be upfront here; the jury will think of it anyway. This is a hackathon build, and we know what it takes to put it in someone's home. Legal basis: under straffeloven § 205 a party to a call may record or process it. So the elderly person must be the one who knowingly turns it on, not a relative installing it secretly. We want consent in their own words and a visible listening indicator. No audio is stored. The transcript lives only in memory; we keep the verdict, the short reason and the time. GDPR: a DPIA, processor agreements with Deepgram and Anthropic, and a documented balancing test for the caller's data. EU endpoints: Deepgram has an EU endpoint; for Claude we route through an EU cloud region or sign zero data retention. Security: today's prototype skips TLS certificate checks and has keys in flash. Before a pilot, the box only talks to our backend with its own credentials. And if the network drops, the light shows "not protected", never a false green. ~50 s.
